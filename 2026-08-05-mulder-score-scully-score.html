Mulder Score, Scully Score

2026-08-05

On August 5 Helix and I started a two-day sprint to expand the UAP casebook. The target was 100 cases. By that afternoon we had 25. "Our target was 100 cases. Why do we only have 25?" I asked. Fair. Every case needs local copies of its source documents, not just links, and that part is slow. "Ok. Rock on until You get to 100 casss," I told the agent, typos intact.

The casebook is the archive we run at fieldreportarchive.com. Its rules are printed in the README: claims are not evidence; preserve the original artifact and its provenance; separate source text, extracted facts, hypotheses, and editorial conclusions; label uncertainty plainly. The data model enforces the separation:

REQUIRED_CASE_FIELDS = {
    'id': str,
    'title': str,
    'category': str,
    'dates': list,
    'locations': list,
    'synopsis': str,
    'status': str,
    'claim': str,
    'evidence_strength': str,
    'mulder_score': int,
    'scully_score': int,
    'hypotheses': list,
    'open_questions': list,
    'sources': list
}

The two scores are the house system. Mulder score: how strong the anomalous reading is. Scully score: how well the conventional explanation covers it. Rendlesham Forest is 7 and 4. The USS Nimitz tic tac is 9 and 3. Roswell gets prose instead of numbers, strong for the documented announcement and the balloon-and-radar-reflector recovery, weak for the extraterrestrial vehicle or the bodies.

The validator also recursively rejects em dashes in any user-facing string. That rule is mine, and the code enforces it on every field.

By the end of August 6 the archive had 104 published cases. Fifty are UFO/UAP proper. The rest are government reports, hearing material, a Starlink sighting, a jet pack sighting, two cryptids. Only 16 carry the full evidence-dossier status, which requires at least three inspectable records plus a timeline and contradictions. Everything else is labeled exactly as under-evidenced as it is.
